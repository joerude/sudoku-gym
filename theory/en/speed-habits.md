# Speed habits

Speed in sudoku is not quick eyes but the absence of empty pauses and redoing. Time is lost in three places: a slow start on a nearly empty grid, chaotic searching with no order, and fixing mistakes that hardcore does not highlight. Below are habits that close all three.

## The rhythm of a game

1. Start: 2–3 seconds to judge which digit is most common. Then straight into scanning that digit across all boxes.
2. Every move triggers a second pass: the same digit across the neighbouring boxes, then the row, column and box of the filled cell.
3. When a digit runs dry, take the next most common one. Don't go back to a digit you just checked until you have placed something new.
4. In the second half of the game, when units are full, switch to naked singles and Full House: the last cells of rows and boxes.
5. If you go by the number of empty cells: with 50 empty and a 4-minute goal, the budget is under 5 seconds a cell. A 20–30 second pause eats the budget of five moves.

## When to switch to notes

- A full 1–9 scan gave no move at all — switch to Snyder notes. A second identical scan "just in case" usually gives nothing.
- Don't write full candidates over the whole grid. Full candidates only in a unit with 3–5 empty cells, when looking for a pair.
- If notes are on the grid, after each move look first at that digit's marks: the second mark in a box often gives a single at once.

## Checking before entering in hardcore

The one-second rule: before pressing a digit, check it in turn in the cell's row, column and box — about a second per unit. One question: "is this digit not here yet?". That is three seconds against a mistake that in hardcore surfaces as a contradiction a few moves later and costs tens of seconds of searching.

- Check the cell too: is it the one you found? Being one cell off when moving with the arrows is a common source of wrong digits.
- If in doubt, don't place it. Move to another digit: a doubtful move will either be confirmed later or turn out false.

## How not to lose time on fixes

- A contradiction (two equal digits in a unit or a cell with no options) — stop. Suspect the last 2–3 digits you placed first.
- If moves can be undone, roll back to the last move you are sure of instead of erasing cells one by one.
- Don't carry on "on top of" a mistake you have spotted: every move that relies on it is wrong too.

## Keyboard input

- One hand on the arrows, the other on the number row. Don't touch the mouse at all.
- Keep the notes-mode toggle on one key or combination — check what your trainer supports, and don't switch with the mouse.
- Move by the shortest path; once you have found a move, first say to yourself "r3c2, five", then move the cursor.

## Typical mistakes

- A long look at the empty grid before the first move. The order is known in advance: the most common digit, boxes 1→9.
- Speeding up at the cost of checking. Three seconds saved on input come back as a minute of hunting for the mistake.
- Looking for hard techniques in medium when a single was missed.

## Practice

Play three medium games in hardcore in a row. In each, watch only two things: the second pass after every move and the "row-column-box" check before every entry. After the game, look at the longest pause and the number of wrong entries in the review. The week's goal is zero wrong entries, and only then a lower time.
